# PDR-001 — Product Experience Model

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-08-18
**Decision owner:** Explore Luxembourg 360

## Decision

Explore Luxembourg 360 will be **exploration-first, powered by community knowledge**.

Discovery of Luxembourg through places, maps, stories, routes, media and immersive experiences is the primary user experience.

Community-generated knowledge, contributions and institutional content will enrich and expand that experience without becoming a mandatory barrier to exploration.

## Product hierarchy

The product is organised conceptually into four layers:

1. **Explore** — the visible primary experience.
2. **Know** — georeferenced knowledge about Luxembourg.
3. **Contribute** — community and partner contributions.
4. **Preserve** — organisation, synchronisation, moderation, storage and long-term preservation of knowledge.

The principle is:

> **Explore → Know → Contribute → Preserve.**

## Rationale

This decision reconciles the current Master Product & Development Prompt with the broader Product Vision.

The Master Prompt establishes exploration as the central user experience: maps, places, stories, media and 360° experiences should make the user feel that they are discovering Luxembourg rather than navigating a database.

The Product Vision also gives significant importance to georeferenced knowledge, community contribution and preservation. These capabilities remain fundamental, but they support the exploration experience rather than becoming the first barrier presented to a new user.

## User experience implications

A first-time visitor should be able to discover content without creating an account or contributing information.

Registered explorers may gain additional capabilities such as:

- favourites;
- saved places;
- collections;
- exploration history;
- personal preferences;
- contributions.

Contributors may provide appropriate content such as photographs, observations, notes or local knowledge, subject to quality, rights, privacy and moderation requirements.

Institutions and approved partners may contribute or validate information through future workflows.

## Strategic consequence

The platform must be valuable before a large community exists. Initial experiences can combine editorial content, official/open data where permitted, external coverage such as Google Street View, and Explore Luxembourg 360 proprietary media.

Community and institutional contributions can progressively increase the richness and authority of the platform without making the core product dependent on immediate network effects.

## Design principle

The interface should communicate:

> **"Where would you like to explore?"**

rather than:

> **"What data would you like to enter?"**

Contribution and knowledge-management capabilities should be available when they are useful, but should not dominate the primary exploration flow.

## Consequences

### Positive

- Stronger first-time user experience.
- Clear product identity centred on exploration.
- Preserves the community and knowledge ambitions of the Product Vision.
- Does not require a large contributor community at launch.
- Allows editorial, official, partner and community content to coexist.

### Negative

- Requires careful information architecture so contribution remains discoverable without becoming intrusive.
- Requires moderation and provenance mechanisms as community content grows.
- Some Product Vision features may belong to later milestones rather than the first release.

## Review trigger

Revisit this decision only if user research, institutional requirements or validated product evidence demonstrates that contribution/knowledge workflows should become the primary entry point for a significant audience.
