**Status:** APPROVED REFINEMENT / RECONCILED

## Reconciliation decision

Typography was already identified as a Design System foundation defined in P6.1. The later work on responsive hierarchy, editorial rhythm, multilingual resilience and content hierarchy therefore belongs as a **P6.1 refinement**, not as a new P6.3 foundation.

No approved design decision is discarded by this renumbering.

## Approved principle

> Typography must create clarity, hierarchy and editorial rhythm while remaining resilient across languages, screen sizes and future content depth.

## Functional hierarchy

1. Display — high-impact moments, used sparingly.
2. H1 / Page Title — primary title.
3. H2 / Section Title — major content sections.
4. H3 / Subsection — local grouping.
5. Body — primary readable content.
6. Supporting — metadata, labels and secondary information.

Hierarchy is created through size, weight, spacing, contrast, width, position and case rather than size alone.

## Responsive guidelines

| Role | Guideline range | Weight | Line-height |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Display | 48–64 px | 600 | ~1.1 |
| H1 | 28–40 px | 600 | ~1.2 |
| H2 | 20–28 px | 600 | ~1.3 |
| H3 | 16–20 px | 600 | ~1.4 |
| Body | 14–18 px | 400 | ~1.6–1.7 |
| Supporting | 12–14 px | 400 | ~1.4–1.6 |

These remain guidelines rather than frozen implementation tokens.

## Editorial rhythm

Typical place-page rhythm may use:

**image → title → metadata → description → map → action → gallery**

The objective is a premium editorial rhythm without turning the application into a continuous text document.

## Content rules

- Important titles wrap naturally rather than being arbitrarily truncated.
- Body text maintains comfortable line length.
- Labels orient rather than decorate.
- Editorial moments are special, not the default.
- Content is written with purpose rather than to fill space.
- Hierarchy remains understandable without relying on colour alone.

## Multilingual requirement

The initial languages are:

- French — Français
- German — Deutsch
- English
- Dutch — Nederlands
- Portuguese — Português
- Luxembourgish — Lëtzebuergesch

Typography must accommodate all six without treating any language as a secondary visual case.

Rollout terminology:

**Initial Full Localisation:** French, German, English, Dutch.

**Progressive Full Localisation:** Portuguese, Luxembourgish.

**Future Expansion:** Italian, Spanish and other languages justified by audience and production capacity.

This describes production rollout, not language importance.

## Content reality and scalability

The hierarchy supports the initial realistic content strategy:

- routes using Google Street View where appropriate;
- own photography of viewpoints, landscapes and places;
- exterior monument content initially.

It also accommodates future own 360° capture, authorised interiors and deeper editorial content without requiring a separate typographic system.

## Font family

The final font family remains open pending validation across all initial languages, special characters, numerals, accessibility, mobile/desktop readability and web performance.
