# P6.3.5 — Typography & Content Hierarchy Audit

**Status:** APPROVED
**Phase:** Design System Foundations Completion
**Decision:** Typography and content hierarchy are approved as a responsive, multilingual editorial system. The final font family remains open for later validation.

## Audit purpose

Validate the approved P6.3.5 direction against the P6.1 visual language, P6.3.1 iconography, P6.3.2 controls, P6.3.3 system states, P6.3.4 motion, mobile behaviour, multilingual requirements and the project's real content-production strategy.

## Approved principle

> Typography must create clarity, hierarchy and editorial rhythm while remaining resilient across languages, screen sizes and future content depth.

Typography guides exploration rather than competing with imagery or place content.

## Functional hierarchy

The system uses six functional levels:

1. **Display** — high-impact moments, used sparingly.
2. **H1 / Page Title** — primary title of the current page or experience.
3. **H2 / Section Title** — major content sections.
4. **H3 / Subsection** — local information grouping.
5. **Body** — primary readable content.
6. **Supporting** — metadata, labels and secondary information.

The hierarchy is not created by size alone. Size, weight, spacing, contrast, width, position and case all contribute.

## Responsive type scale

The visual demonstration provides ranges rather than frozen tokens:

| Role | Guideline range | Weight | Line-height guideline |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Display | 48–64 px | 600 | ~1.1 |
| H1 | 28–40 px | 600 | ~1.2 |
| H2 | 20–28 px | 600 | ~1.3 |
| H3 | 16–20 px | 600 | ~1.4 |
| Body | 14–18 px | 400 | ~1.6–1.7 |
| Supporting | 12–14 px | 400 | ~1.4–1.6 |

These are design guidelines, not final numerical tokens. Exact values will be consolidated during implementation and responsive validation.

## Editorial rhythm

Place pages should use a deliberate rhythm rather than continuous text blocks. Typical structures may alternate:

**image → title → metadata → description → map → action → gallery**

This supports the project's premium, book-like identity while remaining practical for an application.

## Photography relationship

Images remain protagonists. Text over imagery is allowed only when contrast and readability are reliable and the text is short enough to preserve the visual role of the image.

Where appropriate, the preferred structure is:

**image → title → context**

rather than placing large amounts of text over photography.

## Metadata

Metadata should remain compact and scannable, e.g.:

**12.4 km · 3h 20 · Moderate**

or, where more detail is useful, structured fields such as Distance, Estimated time, Difficulty, Elevation and Category.

The system must not turn every piece of metadata into a separate card.

## Content rules

- Important titles wrap naturally rather than being arbitrarily truncated.
- Body text should maintain comfortable line length.
- Labels orient rather than decorate.
- Editorial moments are special, not the default.
- Content is written with purpose rather than to fill visual space.
- The hierarchy must remain understandable without relying on colour alone.

## Multilingual architecture

The initial full product localisation languages are:

- French — Français
- German — Deutsch
- English
- Dutch — Nederlands
- Portuguese — Português
- Luxembourgish — Lëtzebuergesch

These six languages must be treated equally by the interface and typography system. The design must accommodate differences in word length and line expansion without breaking hierarchy.

### Rollout terminology

The previous conceptual Tier 1 / Tier 2 language grouping is intentionally replaced by rollout terminology to avoid implying that any language is more important than another:

**Initial Full Localisation**
- French
- German
- English
- Dutch

**Progressive Full Localisation**
- Portuguese
- Luxembourgish

**Future Expansion**
- Italian
- Spanish
- other languages justified by audience and production capacity

This describes production rollout, not cultural or user importance.

## Content scalability

The typography system supports the project's actual launch content:

- exterior photography;
- Google Street View experiences;
- routes;
- basic place information;
- viewpoints and monuments.

It also supports future depth without redesign:

- own 360° capture;
- interior experiences when authorised;
- deeper historical/cultural editorial content;
- additional experiences and media.

The architecture is prepared for future content depth while the initial content remains honest to what can actually be produced.

## Font family

**Open for later decision.** The final family must be validated for:

- all initial languages;
- accents and special characters;
- numerals and units;
- mobile and desktop readability;
- accessibility;
- web performance.

The typography direction is approved independently of the final font selection.

## Accessibility

The hierarchy is designed not to depend solely on colour or size. Semantic structure, spacing, position, contrast and text labels contribute to meaning.

Final technical accessibility validation remains part of **P6.3.6 — Accessibility**.
