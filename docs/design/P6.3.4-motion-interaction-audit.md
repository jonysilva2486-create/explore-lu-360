# P6.3.4 — Motion & Interaction Audit

**Status:** APPROVED
**Phase:** Design System Foundations Completion
**Decision:** Motion language approved. The enter/exit rule has been refined to prevent unnecessary animation, and motion tokens/patterns remain subject to later consolidation.

## Audit purpose

Validate the approved P6.3.4 Motion & Interaction direction against the P6.1 visual language, P6.3.1 iconography, P6.3.2 controls, P6.3.3 system states, mobile behaviour, 360° interaction and performance requirements.

## Core principle

> **Motion should explain, connect, orient and provide feedback — never entertain.**

Motion is used only when it improves understanding, continuity, orientation or interaction feedback. If movement does not improve the experience, it should not be used.

## Refined enter / exit rule

> **Elements entering or leaving may animate only when the movement communicates hierarchy, continuity, spatial relationship or interaction state.**

This explicitly does **not** mean that every entering or leaving element should animate.

Examples where motion is justified:

- Map marker → place preview, when spatial continuity is preserved.
- Place → 360° experience, when the transition communicates a change from information to immersive space.
- Bottom sheet: closed → peek → expanded, when movement communicates hierarchy.
- Save → Saved, when the transition communicates a state change.

Examples where motion should normally be avoided:

- Every card appearing on a page.
- Every list item animating on scroll.
- Decorative elements entering the viewport without a functional reason.
- Content that should appear immediately and predictably.
- Repeated animations that increase perceived waiting time.

## Motion model

### Primary functions

1. **Orient** — help the user understand where they are and where they are going.
2. **Connect** — show continuity between related states or experiences.
3. **Feedback** — confirm that an interaction or state change occurred.

### Timing guidelines

- Fast: approximately 120–160 ms
- Standard: approximately 200–280 ms
- Emphasis: approximately 350–450 ms

These are guidelines, not frozen final numerical tokens.

### Easing categories

- Standard
- Entrance
- Exit
- Spatial

The system intentionally avoids a large collection of custom easing curves.

## Spatial motion

Spatial motion is particularly relevant to Explore Luxembourg 360 because the product combines maps, places, routes, photography, Street View and future 360° experiences.

Map → Place and Place → 360° transitions may use spatial continuity when it improves orientation.

360° motion must respect user control. Automatic camera movement, unnecessary rotation and intrusive hotspot animation should be avoided.

## Bottom sheets

Bottom sheets may use motion to communicate:

**Closed → Peek → Expanded → Collapsed**

The movement should feel native to the interface and communicate hierarchy rather than behave like a decorative animation.

## Micro-interactions

Micro-interactions should be short, functional and restrained.

Example:

**Save → Saving → Saved**

The motion confirms the state transition; it does not celebrate the action.

## Loading

Loading motion should remain calm and should not make waiting feel longer. Skeletons and progressive loading may use subtle motion when useful, but motion must never replace clear system-state communication.

## System-state integration

P6.3.3 defines what is happening; P6.3.4 defines how the transition may be perceived.

Motion must not turn errors, offline states, permission states or unavailable content into theatrical animations.

## Reduced Motion

When reduced-motion preferences are active:

- non-essential animation is reduced or removed;
- spatial transitions may be simplified;
- functional feedback remains available;
- information must never depend solely on movement.

Detailed accessibility thresholds and validation remain part of P6.3.6.

## Performance principle

> **Responsiveness > animation.**

Motion must never make the application feel slower, particularly around maps, imagery, Street View, 360° content and mobile interactions.

## Motion architecture

The implementation should follow:

**Motion Tokens → Motion Patterns → Components**

This prevents individual components or developers from inventing unrelated animation behaviour.

## Audit conclusion

**P6.3.4 — Motion & Interaction is APPROVED BY AUDIT.**

The enter/exit animation rule has been explicitly refined to prevent motion creep:

> **Elements entering or leaving may animate only when the movement communicates hierarchy, continuity, spatial relationship or interaction state.**

Motion is therefore a functional part of the Explore Luxembourg 360 experience, not a decorative layer.

Final numerical Motion Tokens and reusable Motion Patterns will be consolidated later in the Design System implementation.
